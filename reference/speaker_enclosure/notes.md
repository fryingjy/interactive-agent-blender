# Reference: small desktop speaker enclosure

Genuinely unseen for this milestone — no prep files existed for this object before this session,
and unlike the Bottle/Flashlight/Mug (all revolved/cylindrical forms), this is a boxy, rectilinear
form with a circular through-cutout, a deliberately different topology challenge.

- Body: a rounded rectangular box, roughly 3.0 wide (X) x 2.0 deep (Y) x 3.6 tall (Z), corners
  softened with a modest bevel — not sharp, not heavily rounded (a desktop speaker, not a pebble).
- Front face (the -Y or +Y face, whichever ends up facing the viewer after initial placement):
  a circular speaker-driver cutout, roughly 1.6 in diameter, centered left-right, placed in the
  upper-middle area of the front face (not dead center vertically — a bit above the visual middle,
  matching how real desktop speakers are usually built, with the driver above a lower cabinet
  volume).
- The cutout should read as a real hole/recess (a driver would sit in it), not just a flat
  decorative circle on the surface — exact depth and construction approach (recessed inset vs.
  through-hole vs. boolean) is NOT pre-specified; that is a genuine judgment call for this session,
  the same way the Mug's handle attachment method was left open.
- A modest back-panel detail is optional, not required — a bare, flat back is an acceptable
  finished state if time/decisions are better spent getting the primary form and the driver cutout
  right.
- Symmetry: left-right mirror symmetric (X axis), front/back and top/bottom are NOT symmetric with
  each other (front has the driver cutout, back doesn't; the driver sits above center, not at
  center).
