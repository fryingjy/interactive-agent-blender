### **#78: Bevel Modifier + Harden Normals**
* **Timestamps:** 70:40 – 71:41

* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique:** Fixes flat-surface shading distortions (gradients/bubbles) on chamfered mechanical parts by aligning the split vertex normals of flat face polygons perpendicular to their geometric plane, mimicking custom weighted vertex normals automatically.

### **#79: Custom Beveled Edges (Bevel Weight)**
* **Timestamps:** 71:42 – 73:25

* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique:** Provides complete artistic control over bevel hierarchy (e.g., large primary silhouette roundings vs. tight panel seams) on a single procedural modifier.

### **#80: Multiple Bevel Modifiers (Vertex Groups)**
* **Timestamps:** 73:26 – 76:08
* **Demonstration:** Stacking multiple Bevel Modifiers on a single object where global Angle or Weight limit methods conflict. Creating **Vertex Groups** (`Ctrl + G`), assigning specific edge vertex selections to them, and setting individual Bevel Modifiers' Limit Method to **Vertex Group**. Loop cuts are used where necessary to isolate adjacent vertices.
* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique:** Solves complex topologies where different edge sets require distinct segment counts, profiles, or widths on the same solid body without baking geometry.

### **#81: Clean Hard-Surface Sub-D Modeling (Crease + Subsurf + Bevel Stack)**
* **Timestamps:** 76:09 – 77:57
* **Demonstration:** 
  1. Setting sharp edges on low-poly geometry using **Mean Crease = 1.0** (or `Shift + E`) alongside **Mean Bevel Weight**.
  2. Adding a **Subdivision Surface Modifier** (`Ctrl + 2`).
  3. Adding a **Bevel Modifier** *after/below* the Subdivision modifier with Limit Method set to **Weight** and **Harden Normals** enabled (with **Auto Smooth** active).
* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique (Workflow Confirmation):**
  * *Standard policy note:* While a traditional Sub-D workflow often places Bevel before Sub-D (to subdivide rounded corners), this technique demonstrates the inverse: **Subdividing the creased base mesh first to establish high-density curved surfaces, and then running a Weighted Bevel on the resulting edge to create a razor-sharp, distortion-free mechanical fillet with hardened normals.** This avoids pinched polygon shading on curved hard-surface hulls without manually inserting holding edge loops.

### **#82: Beveled Booleans via Weld Modifier**
* **Timestamps:** 77:58 – 80:05
* **Demonstration:** Setting up a Boolean difference cut, followed by a **Bevel Modifier** set to Angle. Because tiny sliver edges and near-coincident vertices prevent the Bevel from expanding (due to *Clamp Overlap*), a **Weld Modifier** is inserted into the stack between the Boolean and the Bevel:
  $$\text{Stack Order: } \text{Boolean} \longrightarrow \text{Weld (small distance threshold, e.g. 0.01--0.05 cm)} \longrightarrow \text{Bevel}$$
* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique:** Procedurally merges microscopic zero-length edges and near-miss vertices generated at boolean intersection boundaries, allowing subsequent procedural Bevel modifiers to fillet the cut seam cleanly without manual cleanup or mesh collapse.

---

### **#83: Slice Cuts (Boolean + Solidify + Weld + Bevel)**
* **Timestamps:** 80:06 – 82:39
* **Demonstration:** Using an open/planar cutter object with a **Solidify Modifier** to cut thin slice gaps into a curved mechanical body via Boolean Difference. The modifier stack on the host object:
  $$\text{Subdivision Surface} \longrightarrow \text{Boolean (Cutter)} \longrightarrow \text{Weld} \longrightarrow \text{Bevel (Angle/Harden Normals)}$$
* **Transferable Hard-Surface Technique:** Produces non-destructive, complex sci-fi panel seams and armor separation lines on curved surfaces while keeping cutters easily repositionable.
