# Polygon Runway beginner ramen-machine reproduction

This run reproduces the modeled artifact from Polygon Runway's 71:45 beginner tutorial at
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K7__BjW4UWE`. It is apprenticeship evidence, not evidence of
unassisted prop-modeling mastery.

## What was actually studied

- YouTube oEmbed bound the creator and video ID.
- `gemini_full_video_unverified.json` records full audiovisual extraction from the same video ID.
- The observed workflow includes a beveled cube housing, horizontal loop cuts, a recessed service
  area, top trim, sign frame, side assemblies, an 18-vertex bowl, curve cables, two stools, food
  details, Japanese signage, materials, emission, and lighting.
- The extraction calls the in-video lesson a Blender 4.0 ramen-shop-machine tutorial while oEmbed
  currently advertises it as a 2026 Blender 5.0 beginner tutorial. The discrepancy is preserved.
- Independent visual comparison was limited to the source thumbnail. The third-party thumbnail is
  retained locally under the ignored `media/` directory and is not redistributed in Git.

## Closed-loop modeling evidence

The main housing begins as one box and remains one connected mesh. Horizontal loop cuts provide
the regions used for the upper sign, service recess, and lower cabinet; visible assemblies remain
separate objects when they have real material, construction, or silhouette boundaries.

The run preserves five useful failures:

1. A face ID captured before beveling disappeared after topology changed, so the attempted inset
   had no valid semantic selection.
2. A technically successful bevel used two horizontal edge IDs and produced the wrong corner form.
   The corrected pass selects all four vertical housing edges.
3. Insetting the entire front face produced a valid but visually oversized recess. Two loop cuts and
   a localized middle-face recess replaced it.
4. The first revolved stool-seat profile used a zero radius and was rejected by the typed all-quad
   revolver. A small positive center radius produced a closed editable surface.
5. Enlarging the bowl improved its proportion but caused a lighting/readability regression in
   material v3. A bounded front fill restored the stools, buttons, and front hierarchy in v4.

`ramen_machine_tutorial_final.blend` is the accepted technical result. Modifiers remain live and
unapplied. `independent_verification.json` was generated by reopening that saved file in a fresh
Blender 5.2 process; all structural checks pass.

## Honest assessment

The final render is recognizable and structurally coherent, with a connected manifold housing,
clear component hierarchy, readable signage, and retained live bevels. Against the available source
thumbnail it is approximately **6.8/10**, not the required 8/10: proportions and several secondary
forms are simplified, source step frames were not independently inspected, and the top basin still
contains two covered n-gons whose viewport tessellation was visible before the bowl was installed.
This lesson therefore does not unlock intermediate work or the held-out prop gate.
