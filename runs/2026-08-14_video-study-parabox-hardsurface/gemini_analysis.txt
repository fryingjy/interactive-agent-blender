### 1. Non-Destructive Edge Beveling via `Bevel Weight`
* **Timestamp:** `05:15 – 05:35`
* **Visible on screen:** In Edit Mode, the 4 vertical corner edges of the main body box are highlighted in cyan. In the right-hand `Item / Transform` sidebar, the artist changes `Mean Bevel Weight` from `0.00` to `1.00`. In the Bevel modifier settings, `Limit Method` is changed from *Angle* to *Weight*.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Instead of applying a destructive `Ctrl + B` bevel directly onto the raw mesh, the artist assigns full bevel weight to specific edges and drives them via a modifier. This keeps the base geometry lightweight and allows real-time tuning of width and segment counts at any later point without destroying topology.
* **Transferable Lesson:** When creating hard-surface enclosures, chassis, or electronic housings, weighting primary silhouette edges allows you to iterate on edge sharpness without committing to high-density geometry early.

### 2. Modifier Stack Ordering for Clean Boolean Seams
* **Timestamp:** `08:00 – 08:15`
* **Visible on screen:** After adding a thin cutting plane to create a horizontal seam across the main body, the cut has harsh, razor-sharp edges. The artist navigates to the modifier stack and drags the `Boolean` modifier directly above/below the `Bevel` modifier.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Placing the Bevel modifier *after* the Boolean operation forces Blender to calculate edge chamfers on the newly generated intersection edges created by the cut, giving the split panel a manufactured, rounded gap rather than an infinitely sharp CG slice.
* **Transferable Lesson:** In mechanical modeling (e.g., panel lines on vehicles or sci-fi armor), placing bevels after booleans in the modifier stack automatically creates realistic tooling seams without manual edge-loop cutting.

### 3. Preventing Sub-D Surface Pinching on Circular Caps via Insetting
* **Timestamp:** `16:25 – 16:50`
* **Visible on screen:** A flat circular button cap deformed under a Subdivision Surface modifier displays radial pinching/warping towards its center. The artist selects the circular front face in Edit Mode and presses `I` twice to create two concentric interior inset loops.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Subdivision surface algorithms struggle on large N-gons and flat poles, pulling the center vertices unevenly. Insetting creates perimeter concentric ring loops that constrain curvature to the border while maintaining a flat, distortion-free circular face.
* **Transferable Lesson:** Whenever modeling flat cylindrical caps (dials, knobs, lenses, exhaust ports) that use sub-d smoothing, always inset the end-faces to isolate the flat center from edge-rounding forces.

### 4. Wireframe-to-Volume Modeling using the Skin Modifier
* **Timestamp:** `30:15 – 30:45`
* **Visible on screen:** A single chain of extruded vertices outlining the arm path. Upon adding a `Skin` modifier, massive blocky polygonal geometry envelopes the path. In Edit Mode, the artist selects all vertices and presses `Ctrl + A` (Skin Resize), scaling down the envelope radius along the chain before adding a Subdivision Surface modifier.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Rather than extruding and rotating 3D cylinders along curves, the artist uses single-vertex edge strings with variable thickness control at each joint/node, turning simple poly-lines into smooth tubular limbs.
* **Transferable Lesson:** Ideal for rapid generation of variable-thickness tubular geometry such as cables, piping, organic limbs, electrical wiring harnesses, and antennas.

### 5. Fixing Skin Modifier Directional Glitches with `Mark Root`
* **Timestamp:** `31:02 – 31:18`
* **Visible on screen:** The connection point where the arm meets the torso produces twisted, crumpled geometry because Blender assigned the root calculation to the second vertex instead of the shoulder joint. The artist selects the top vertex and clicks the `Mark Root` button in the Skin modifier tab, instantly straightening the mesh.
* **Action & Reasoning:** The Skin modifier evaluates branching and local orientation relative to a single designated root vertex. Re-centering the root to the anchor point fixes inverted polygon calculation.
* **Transferable Lesson:** Essential troubleshooting step when using the Skin modifier for branching objects (e.g., pipes with split tees, tree branches, fingers attached to hands).

### 6. Diagnosing and Fixing Inverted Normals via `Face Orientation`
* **Timestamp:** `47:00 – 47:30`
* **Visible on screen:** The artist opens the Viewport Overlays dropdown and enables `Face Orientation`. The model turns blue except for the cartridge slot boolean cutter, which displays in bright red. The artist enters Edit Mode on the cutter, selects all geometry (`A`), and presses `Shift + N` (`Recalculate Outside`), turning the faces blue.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Red faces signify inverted normals (pointing inward). Inverted normals cause booleans to subtract incorrectly, generate dark lighting artifacts, and break shading.
* **Transferable Lesson:** Checking face orientation overlay is a critical diagnostic step in any hard-surface pipeline before finalizing booleans, texture baking, or export to game engines.

---

### 7. Eliminating Boolean Shading Artifacts with `Harden Normals`
* **Timestamp:** `52:30 – 53:20`
* **Visible on screen:** Applying a small corner bevel across the speaker vent boolean cutouts produces dark diagonal shading streaks across the surrounding flat body panels. In the Bevel modifier -> `Shading` rollout, the artist checks the box for `Harden Normals`. The dark shading gradients instantly vanish, leaving flat surfaces completely clean.
* **Action & Reasoning:** Booleans on curved/beveled surfaces create messy underlying triangulation that twists vertex normals across flat faces. `Harden Normals` transfers the true flat vector normal of the large faces onto the beveled boundary vertices without requiring manual retopology.
* **Transferable Lesson:** This is the core technique in modern mid-poly hard-surface modeling (weapons, industrial props, vehicles), allowing complex boolean cutouts on hard surfaces without requiring tedious manual quad clean-up.
